## 1) Results (set-valued; no single “winner”)

### 1.1 Robust Pareto frontier (v0.1; framework-level, mixed evidence)
**Frontier set:** `{F1 Memetics-as-replicators, F2 Dual-inheritance/cultural evolution, F3 Epidemiology/attraction}`. [S01] [S02]

Rationale (high level):
- Each framing has at least one plausible advantage under the declared indicators, and none is **robustly** at least-as-good on all indicators relative to the others under the coarse ordinal/qual rubric. [S02] [S01]

### 1.2 Context-conditional notes (where dominance might emerge)
- In an **online token-diffusion bounded context** (hashtags/retweets; limited attention; complex contagion), F2 and F1 are typically more directly operationalizable via observable proxies, while F3 may require additional measurement work to connect surface traces to representations. [S24] [S26] [S18]
- In a **transformative-transmission bounded context** (systematic reconstruction; attractor dynamics), F3 is often better aligned with the primary objection to strong replicator memetics (copying vs re-production), while F1 risks overcommitment if fidelity/unit identity cannot be defended. [S28] [S22] [S29]

These are *not* global rankings; they are scope notes to inform the hypothesis/problem portfolios. [S01] [S02]

## 2) Candidates (fixed; from parity plan)

Definition used here: culture explained as distributions of representations; transformative transmission and attractor-like convergence are central. [S18] [S22] [S29]

## 4) Dominance analysis (why no single winner)

### 4.1 Robust dominance claims (none in v0.1)
No framing is robustly ≥ on all indicators relative to another under the coarse rubric and current evidence window. [S02]

### 4.2 Key trade-offs driving non-dominance
- **Falsifiability vs unit/proxy ease**:
  - F1 can be highly falsifiable *if* it commits to a strong meme construct, but that same commitment creates unit-identity and transformation vulnerabilities. [S28] [S29]
  - F2 achieves broad proxy applicability without discrete-unit overcommitment, but risks underdetermination if mechanisms/models are not pinned. [S14] [S05]
- **Transmission transformation alignment vs measurement burden**:
  - F3 is best aligned with transformative transmission objections to memetics, but may impose heavier measurement/microfoundation burdens in some domains. [S22] [S18] [S24]

### 4.3 “Possible dominance” (scope-bounded; not global)
- In strictly **token diffusion** contexts using observable proxies (hashtags) and attention constraints, F2 may be *possibly* superior to F1 by avoiding strong unit-ontology commitments while retaining tractability. [S24] [S14] [S28]
- In contexts where **transformative reconstruction** is empirically central, F3 may be *possibly* superior to F1 if strong replicator assumptions fail. [S28] [S22] [S29]

These are conditional hypotheses, not settled conclusions. [S01] [S02]
